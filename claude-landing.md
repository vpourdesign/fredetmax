# CLAUDE-LANDING.md — Performance Landing Page Rules

## Always Do First
- **Invoke the `frontend-design` skill** before writing any frontend code, every session, no exceptions.

## What a Landing Page Is
A performance landing page has one job: convert. Every decision — layout, color, type, spacing, motion — serves that goal. It is not a portfolio piece. It is not a design showcase. It is a conversion machine that happens to look beautiful.

## Step 1 — Identify the Field
Before writing any code, understand the industry and audience. Mentally reference the best-in-class landing pages in that space. Ask: how do top players in this field — whether real estate, construction, health, SaaS, legal, retail, or anything else — build trust and drive action? What visual language, tone, and section structure do they use? Internalize that standard. Express it originally.

## Step 2 — Choose an Approach
Before writing any code, identify which approach the page calls for. **If the user doesn't specify, ask.** The wrong approach for the audience will kill conversions regardless of how well-designed the page is.

---

### 🔬 Scientific / Authority Approach
**Tone:** Authoritative, precise, credible. Speaks to the rational brain.
**Best for:** Health, supplements, legal, financial services, B2B, medical devices, technical products, anything where trust is built through proof and expertise.

**Design language:**
- Clean, structured layout. Generous whitespace. Nothing cluttered.
- Neutral or cool-tinted backgrounds (white, off-white, pale grey-blue). Brand color used sparingly as an accent.
- Sans-serif primary font. Serif used only for pull quotes or key statistics.
- Numbers and data presented as designed callout elements — not buried in paragraphs.
- Credential badges, certifications, regulatory info, or professional affiliations prominently placed.
- Diagrams, process steps, or mechanism explainers presented as clean iconographic sections.

**Copy patterns:**
- Lead with a specific, credible claim backed by a number: "94% client satisfaction over 12 years."
- Name the process or differentiator: "Our 3-phase methodology ensures..."
- Cite proof inline: "Verified by [authority], [year]."
- Objections answered factually, not emotionally.
- Headlines are clear and direct, not poetic.

**Section order:** Hero claim → Trust bar (credentials, certifications, awards) → Process / methodology explainer → Evidence / results → Offer spotlight → Reviews → FAQ → CTA

### 💛 Emotional / Storytelling Approach
**Tone:** Warm, human, intimate. Speaks to the feeling brain first, justifies with logic second.
**Best for:** Lifestyle brands, wellness, beauty, home services, personal challenges, luxury, anything where the customer needs to feel *understood* before they'll buy.

**Design language:**
- Warm, organic backgrounds (cream, warm grey, soft brand tint). Rich brand color used boldly.
- Serif or display font dominates headlines — evocative, editorial feel.
- Photography or imagery-forward. People, texture, context — not product or service on white.
- Sections flow like a narrative, not a feature list. Each builds emotional momentum.
- Pull quotes from real customers in large type. Let them carry entire sections.
- Micro-details that signal craft: subtle texture, organic shapes, handcrafted-feel accents.

**Copy patterns:**
- Open with the problem as a lived experience: "You've been putting it off. The right moment never comes."
- Use "you" constantly. The reader is the hero. The product or service is the tool.
- Transition from pain → possibility → solution naturally — never abruptly.
- Social proof is human: full name, photo, specific story. Not just stars.
- Headlines are evocative: "Finally, a home you're proud of." not "Premium renovation services."
- End with hope and urgency, not just a price.

**Section order:** Hero (empathy hook) → "You're not alone" moment → Journey / transformation → Solution reveal → Proof (reviews with photos) → Guarantee → Final emotional CTA

### 📋 Lead Generation Approach
**Tone:** Confident, reassuring, low-pressure. Speaks to someone who is interested but not ready to buy — they need to trust you enough to hand over their contact information.
**Best for:** Real estate, construction, renovation, legal, financial advisory, insurance, consulting, home services, clinics, agencies — any field where the sale happens offline, through a human conversation, after the form is submitted.

**The core dynamic:** The visitor is not being asked to buy. They are being asked to take a small, safe step. Every element on the page exists to make that step feel obvious, low-risk, and worthwhile. The form is the finish line, not a distraction.

**Design language:**
- Warm but professional. Trustworthy without being cold. Approachable without being casual.
- Strong hero with a clear value proposition tied directly to what they get by filling the form: "Get your free estimate", "Speak to an expert today", "Find out what your property is worth."
- The lead form is always visible — either embedded in the hero section or in a sticky sidebar on desktop. Never buried at the bottom.
- Authority signals dominate the top half: years in business, number of clients served, awards, recognizable partner logos, professional certifications.
- Real photos of the team, the office, or completed work. People buy from people. Stock photos kill trust.
- Reviews are specific and outcome-focused: "They got back to me within the hour and the quote was exactly what they said." — not vague praise.
- Color used to direct attention: brand color on the form's CTA button only. Everything else is neutral.

**The form itself:**
- 3 fields maximum above the fold: name, phone or email, and one qualifying field (e.g. "Type of project", "City", "Budget range").
- Label every field clearly. No placeholder-only labels — they disappear when the user starts typing.
- CTA on the form is specific: "Obtenir ma soumission gratuite", "Parler à un expert", "Réserver mon appel" — not "Submit."
- Below the form button: a micro-reassurance line. "No obligation. We respond within 24 hours." or "Your information is never shared."
- On mobile: the form becomes a full-width sticky bottom sheet or a floating "Get a quote" button that opens a modal.
- Never ask for more information than necessary to qualify the lead. Every extra field reduces conversions.

**Authority signals — stack as many as apply:**
- Years in business: "15 ans d'expérience"
- Volume: "500+ projets complétés", "1 200 familles accompagnées"
- Geographic credibility: "Serving the Greater Montreal area since 2009"
- Certifications, licenses, professional memberships with badge-style display
- Awards: "Best of Houzz 2023", "Prix Reconnaissance de l'industrie"
- Media mentions or press logos
- Insurance and bonding confirmation — critical for construction and legal
- Response time promise: "We respond within 2 business hours"

**Social proof — format matters:**
- Use full name + city + project type: "Marie L. — Laval — Rénovation cuisine"
- Star rating displayed visually (filled stars, not just "5/5")
- Quote focused on a specific fear overcome or result achieved — not generic praise
- If possible, before/after imagery paired with the review
- Video testimonials outperform text by a wide margin — use a thumbnail + play button placeholder if no real video exists
- Show the total review count prominently: "4.9 ★ · 312 avis vérifiés"

**Objection-killing FAQ — answer these specifically:**
- "How quickly will you respond?"
- "Is this really free / no obligation?"
- "Do you serve my area?"
- "How long does the process take?"
- "What happens after I submit the form?"
- "Are you licensed / insured / certified?"

**Copy patterns:**
- Hero headline names the outcome: "Obtenez une soumission gratuite en 24 heures." not "We offer renovation services."
- Subheadline qualifies and reassures: "No pressure, no commitment. Just an honest conversation about your project."
- Use "you" and "your" constantly. Every sentence should feel personal.
- Social proof pulls double duty — it proves results AND reduces the fear of submitting.
- Urgency is soft and credible: "We take on a limited number of projects per month" — not fake countdown timers.
- Never use aggressive sales language. The form is a door, not a trap.

**Section order:**
Hero (outcome headline + embedded form) → Authority bar (logos, years, volume) → Why us / differentiators (3–4 points, icon + short copy) → Social proof (reviews with names and photos) → Process explainer ("Here's what happens after you submit") → FAQ → Repeated form or CTA → Footer

### 📈 ROI / Results Approach
**Tone:** Direct, confident, outcome-focused. Speaks to decision-makers who want value, not feelings.
**Best for:** SaaS, B2B services, construction, real estate investment, consulting, agencies, any product or service where the buyer is thinking about return, risk, or efficiency.

**Design language:**
- Bold, structured layout. Strong typographic hierarchy. Numbers are the heroes.
- Dark or high-contrast hero (dark navy, charcoal, or deep brand color). Light sections below.
- Clean sans-serif throughout. Tight, confident typography.
- Feature/benefit comparison tables or before/after grids.
- Case study callouts with specific outcomes: time saved, revenue added, cost reduced.
- Social proof from recognizable names, companies, or logos.

**Copy patterns:**
- Lead with the outcome, not the product: "Cut project delays by 40%." / "Sell faster, for more."
- Quantify everything: "Average client saves 11 hours per week."
- Name the problem in business terms: "Most contractors lose 15% of margin to rework."
- CTAs are low-risk entry points: "Book a free call", "See a demo", "Get a quote" — not "Buy now."
- Testimonials include role, company, and a specific result.
- FAQ section addresses ROI, timeline, and risk reduction directly.

**Section order:** Hero (bold outcome statement) → Logo bar / credibility → Problem in numbers → Solution + differentiators → Case study / results → Pricing or packages → FAQ → CTA

## Anatomy — Shared Structure
Regardless of approach or field, every landing page needs:
1. **Nav** — logo + single CTA button. No distracting links.
2. **Hero** — headline, subheadline, primary CTA, one social proof element. Above the fold on desktop AND mobile.
3. **Trust bar** — logos, awards, certifications, years in business, or a scrolling ticker of key claims.
4. **Core content** — follows the approach-specific section order above.
5. **Social proof** — reviews or testimonials with names. 3–6 is enough. More is noise.
6. **FAQ** — 4–6 objection-killers. Accordion.
7. **Final CTA section** — repeat the offer, one big button.
8. **Footer** — minimal. Legal only, nothing that distracts.

## CTA Rules
- One primary CTA color. Used only for CTA buttons — nowhere else on the page.
- CTA appears: in the nav, in the hero, after social proof, at the end. Minimum 3× per page.
- CTA copy is active, specific, and field-appropriate:
  - Product: "Commander maintenant", "Essayer gratuitement"
  - Service: "Obtenir une soumission", "Réserver un appel", "Demander une démo"
  - Real estate / construction: "Voir les projets", "Obtenir une évaluation gratuite"
  - Never "Submit" or "Click here."
- On mobile: sticky CTA bar at the bottom of the screen. Always.
- Button shape: rounded (border-radius 8–50px depending on brand feel). Never sharp corners.

## Visual Hierarchy Rules
- **One brand color.** Derive a dark and light variant. That is the palette.
- Background: off-white (`#fafafa`) or brand-tinted. Never pure `#ffffff` — too harsh.
- Headline in a serif or strong display font. Body in a clean sans. Never the same font for both.
- Section rhythm: alternate white and tinted backgrounds. Keeps sections distinct without borders.
- Max content width: `1100px`. Never wider.
- Never center-align body text. Only center headlines and short taglines.

## Typography
- Headline: `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.5rem)`, weight 800–900, `letter-spacing: -0.02em`, `line-height: 1.1`
- Subheadline: `clamp(1rem, 2vw, 1.2rem)`, weight 400, `line-height: 1.65`, muted color, `max-width: 520px`
- Body: `clamp(0.95rem, 1.5vw, 1.05rem)`, `line-height: 1.7`
- Always `clamp()`. Never fixed px font sizes.
- Google Fonts or Bunny Fonts. Two fonts max.

## CSS — Keep It Simple
- **Tailwind CDN** for utilities.
- **Custom CSS** for brand tokens, keyframes, and anything Tailwind can't express cleanly.
- Always define a `:root` block: brand color, dark/light variants, off-white, text color, muted text, radius, spacing.
- Transition only `transform`, `opacity`, `color`, `background-color`, `box-shadow`. Never `transition-all`.
- No external JS animation libraries unless explicitly requested.

## Animations — Minimal and CSS-Only by Default
- **Hover states:** `transform: translateY(-2px)` + shadow lift on buttons and cards. That's it.
- **Entrance animations:** Intersection Observer + single `.is-visible` class: `opacity 0 → 1`, `translateY(12px) → 0`. One utility, used everywhere.
- **Ticker/marquee:** pure CSS `@keyframes`. No JS.
- **FAQ accordion:** vanilla JS class toggle. No library.
- **Sticky nav shadow:** JS scroll listener, one `box-shadow` toggle. Nothing more.
- If it can't be done with CSS transitions and vanilla JS in under 20 lines, it's too complex for a landing page.

## Shadows
Never flat. Never default Tailwind. Always layered and color-tinted:
```css
/* Card */
box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.05), 0 8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.06);

/* CTA button */
box-shadow: 0 4px 14px rgba(VAR_BRAND_RGB, 0.35);

/* Elevated / floating */
box-shadow: 0 8px 32px rgba(0,0,0,.12), 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.06);
```

## Mobile — Non-Negotiable
- Hero fully readable and CTA tappable above the fold at 390px wide.
- Sticky bottom CTA bar on mobile only (`@media (max-width: 768px)`).
- All grids collapse to single column. No horizontal scroll.
- Tap targets minimum 44px tall.

## Trust Signals — Adapt to the Field
Use whichever apply. Never force ones that don't fit the industry:
- Star ratings with review count
- Years in business / number of clients / projects completed
- Awards, certifications, professional memberships
- "As seen in" / media logos
- Recognizable client or partner logos
- Guarantees, warranties, or risk-reversal statements
- Before/after results, case study statistics
- Real customer photos and named testimonials

## Pricing Block (when applicable)
- Show the price or starting price prominently — never hide it.
- If there's a discount, show the crossed-out original.
- Guarantee or refund policy right below the price. Reduces friction.
- Per-unit or per-outcome breakdown builds value perception.
- For service businesses: "Starting at", "Free estimate", or package tiers work better than a single price.

## Local Server
- **Always serve on localhost** — never screenshot a `file:///` URL.
- Start: `node serve.mjs` (serves project root at `http://localhost:3000`)
- If already running, do not start a second instance.

## Screenshot Workflow
- Puppeteer: `C:/Users/nateh/AppData/Local/Temp/puppeteer-test/`. Chrome cache: `C:/Users/nateh/.cache/puppeteer/`.
- **Always screenshot from localhost:** `node screenshot.mjs http://localhost:3000`

- Optional label: `node screenshot.mjs http://localhost:3000 label`
- Read the PNG with the Read tool after every screenshot. Analyze it.
- Minimum 2 screenshot passes. Fix before declaring done.
- Check: hero above the fold, CTA visibility, mobile layout, section contrast, type hierarchy.

## Output Defaults
- Single `index.html`, all styles inline, unless told otherwise.
- Tailwind via CDN: `<script src="https://cdn.tailwindcss.com"></script>`
- Google Fonts via `<link>` in `<head>`.
- Placeholder images: `https://placehold.co/WIDTHxHEIGHT`
- No GSAP, no Lenis, no Motion One — unless explicitly requested.

## Brand Assets
- Always check `brand_assets/` before designing.
- Use any logos, colors, or style guides found there.
- Never invent brand colors if a palette exists. Never use a placeholder where a real asset exists.

## Anti-Generic Guardrails
- Never use default Tailwind blue/indigo as the primary color.
- Never use `shadow-md` or any flat single-stop shadow.
- Never use the same font for headings and body.
- Never center-align body paragraphs.
- Never use more than 2 typefaces.
- Never add animations before layout and content are stable.
- Never use `transition-all`.
- Never use trust signals that don't match the field — a construction company doesn't need "NPN certified."

## Hard Rules
- **Ask which approach (Scientific, Emotional, or ROI) before starting if the user hasn't specified.**
- **Ask about the field and target audience if not clear from context.**
- One page, one goal. No nav links that take users off-page (except footer legal).
- CTA button color appears nowhere else on the page.
- Every section must earn its place — if it doesn't build trust or move toward conversion, cut it.
- A clean, still page that converts beats an animated, cluttered one every time.
- Do not add sections not briefed by the user.
- Do not stop after one screenshot pass.
